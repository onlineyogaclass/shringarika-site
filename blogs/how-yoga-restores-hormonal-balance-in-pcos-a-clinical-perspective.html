The Complete, Detailed Guide to Your Daily Healing Asanas

1. Supported Reclined Butterfly Pose (Supta Baddha Konasana)

Time to Hold: 10 to 12 minutes every single evening, ideally right before you sleep.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Lie down completely flat on a soft yoga mat or right on your bed. Bring the bottoms of your feet together so they touch each other, and let your knees gently fall open wide to the left and right sides. Place thick bed pillows or cushions directly underneath your outer thighs and knees. This support is very important; your inner thighs should feel completely relaxed without any pulling or stretching strain. Rest your palms softly on your lower belly, close your eyes, and let your entire body sink down heavy into the floor.

How it helps your body: This passive position completely takes away all physical tension from your pelvic floor muscles. It calms the hyper-alert signals coming from your adrenal glands, helps reduce fluid pressure around your ovaries, and allows clean, warm circulation to gather in your lower core.

2. Left-Nostril Soothing Breathwork (Chandra Bhedana Pranayama)

Time to Practice: 5 to 7 minutes every morning before you consume any food.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Sit up cross-legged in a comfortable position, using a cushion under your hips so your lower back stays straight and relaxed. Relax your shoulders down away from your ears. Rest your left hand on your thigh. Bring your right hand up toward your nose. Close your eyes softly. Use your right thumb to gently close your right nostril. Breathe in slowly, quietly, and deeply through your open left nostril for a count of 4 seconds. Then, lift your thumb and use your ring finger to gently close your left nostril, exhaling all the air out smoothly through your right nostril for a count of 6 seconds. Repeat this steady loop: always breathing in through the left, and always breathing out through the right.

Why it works: This cooling breathing style serves as an immediate brake for your survival nervous system. It reduces circulating stress chemicals, balances your heart rate variations, and helps open up tiny blood vessels that have been constricted by modern tension.

3. Post-Meal Thunderbolt Posture (Vajrasana)

Time to Hold: 5 minutes straight after finishing your main lunch and dinner meals.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Right after eating, find a flat, comfortable surface. Kneel down on both knees. Bring your two big toes together behind you so they touch, and spread your heels out wide to the sides, making a comfortable saddle shape for your hips to sit down into. Sit tall, keep your spine naturally straight, and rest your hands flat on your thighs. Keep your gaze soft and straight ahead, allowing your stomach to move freely with every breath.

Why it works: Kneeling in Vajrasana alters the blood flow patterns in your legs, redirecting a strong wave of circulation straight into your digestive organs. This boosts your metabolic fire, reduces bloating, and helps your cells process and manage glucose surges from your meals much more efficiently.

4. Supported Legs-Up-The-Wall Posture (Viparita Karani)

Time to Hold: 10 minutes every evening when you return home or feel physically drained.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Set your mat or a soft blanket right next to a clear wall. Sit sideways with one hip touching the wall. Gently roll your spine down flat onto your back while swinging your legs straight up against the wall. Your body will form a simple L-shape. Slide a soft pillow or a folded blanket right under your lower back and tailbone so your hips sit slightly higher than the floor. Let your arms open out to the sides with your palms facing the ceiling, and relax your leg muscles completely.

Why it works: This inverted hold turns around the continuous pull of gravity on your lower body. It helps clear away stagnant fluid accumulation from your pelvis, reduces swelling and heaviness in your legs, and signals your brain to enter a deep state of tissue repair and restoration.
