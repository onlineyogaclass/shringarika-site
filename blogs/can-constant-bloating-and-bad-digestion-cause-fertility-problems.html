The Estrobolome Matrix: How Gut Bacteria Control Estrogen

To understand why bad digestion compromises fertility, we have to look closely at a specialized ecosystem within your gut microbiome known as the estrobolome. The estrobolome consists of a unique group of intestinal bacteria responsible for regulating and balancing the amount of circulating estrogen inside your body.

When your digestion is compromised—whether due to low stomach acid, chronic stress, or poor food pairing—food stalls and ferments in your intestines. This stagnation creates an overgrowth of harmful bacteria, disrupting the estrobolome. These imbalanced bacteria produce an excess enzyme called beta-glucuronidase, which prevents your liver from flushing out spent estrogen waste. This waste is reabsorbed back into your bloodstream, causing estrogen dominance, irregular ovulation patterns, and the formation of cellular Ama (sluggish toxic debris) that can interfere with a healthy uterine lining.
