1. Supported Reclined Butterfly Pose (Supta Baddha Konasana)

Duration: Hold quietly for 10 to 12 minutes every evening or right before sleep.

How to practice: Come down to sit comfortably on your bed or a soft mat. Slowly rest your upper spine and back flat onto the floor. Bring the soles of your feet together to touch, and let your knees gently open wide to the left and right sides. Place thick pillows or rolled blankets right under your outer thighs. This support is absolutely vital so your lower belly and inner groin do not experience any harsh pulling or muscular strain. Rest your hands softly over your lower abdomen, close your eyes, and allow your body to become completely heavy.

2. Soothing Throat-Resonance Breathing (Ujjayi Pranayama Protocol)

Duration: Practice for 5 to 7 minutes every morning on an empty stomach.

How to practice: Sit up completely straight and tall in a comfortable cross-legged position, keeping your spine aligned and your shoulders relaxed. Close your mouth softly. Inhale slowly and deeply through both nostrils while gently narrowing the back of your throat tissue, exactly like the feeling of preparing to whisper. This soft contraction will create a quiet, steady, soothing sound from your throat—resembling soft ocean waves. Maintain that slight throat narrowing as you exhale smoothly and slowly. Keep the sound perfectly even, calm, and continuous.

The Biological Response: The soft friction created by this specialized breathing style directly stimulates your vagus nerve endings located around the larynx. This action quickly down-regulates survival responses, lowers circulating cortisol, and improves your cells' natural metabolic efficiency.

3. Post-Meal Thunderbolt Posture (Vajrasana)

Duration: Hold for 5 minutes immediately within 10 minutes of finishing your main meals.

How to practice: Find a soft mat or carpeted floor. Kneel down on both knees. Bring your big toes together behind you so they touch, and spread your heels out wide to the sides, creating a comfortable space to sit your seat down directly between your feet. Keep your spine long and straight, roll your chest open, and rest your palms flat on your thighs. Look straight ahead and breathe naturally, allowing your belly to rise and fall softly.

The Biological Response: Kneeling in Vajrasana alters the blood circulation paths in your lower body, temporarily redirecting a warm wave of blood flow away from your legs and straight into your digestive organs. This steps up your digestive fire, eliminates post-meal bloating, and assists your body in processing and clearing out glucose from your meal.

4. Supported Legs-Up-The-Wall Posture (Viparita Karani)

Duration: Sit and hold for 10 minutes every evening when you feel physically or mentally fatigued.

How to practice: Place your yoga mat or a soft blanket directly up against an open wall. Sit sideways with one hip pressing against the wall. In one gentle, fluid movement, lower your upper back flat down onto the floor while swinging your legs straight up against the wall. Your body will form a comfortable L-shape. Slide a soft pillow or folded blanket directly under your lower back and hips to keep your pelvis slightly elevated. Relax your arms out to your sides with your palms facing upward, and let all the tension drain from your thighs.

The Biological Response: This posture reverses the daily effects of gravity on your circulatory system. It assists stagnant fluids in draining safely back out of your lower limbs, relieves pelvic congestion, and guides your brain into a state of deep cellular restoration.
