The Complete, Detailed Guide to Your Daily Chandra Bhedana Protocol

1. Establish a Straight, Decompressed Posture

Step-by-Step Instructions: As soon as you feel an internal stress spike rising during your afternoon, sit up beautifully straight in a comfortable chair or in a cross-legged position on your bed. Place a small cushion directly underneath your sitting bones to help decompress your lower back. Pull your shoulders gently back and lift through the crown of your head to stack your spine straight, ensuring your diaphragm has absolute space to expand without constraint. Rest your left hand flat on your knee and drop your gaze down.

2. Form the Mudra and Close Your Right Nostril Safely

Step-by-Step Instructions: Bring your right hand up close to your nose. Close your index and middle fingers down into your palm, leaving your thumb and ring finger extended. Relax your shoulders completely. Use your right thumb to softly and securely press against your right nasal wing, sealing the right nostril entirely closed while keeping your left nostril fully open. Close your eyelids softly.

3. Practice the 1:2 Rhythmic Cooling Breath (5 to 7 Minutes)

Step-by-Step Instructions: Empty your lungs fully. Inhale slowly, quietly, and deeply through your open left nostril for a silent mental count of 4 seconds, letting your lower stomach expand smoothly. Do not hold your breath. Immediately begin to release a quiet, steady, and complete exhalation out through the exact same left nostril for an extended mental count of 8 seconds. Maintain this precise 1:2 ratio continuously—always breathing in for 4 seconds through the left, and always breathing out for 8 seconds through the left. Continue for 5 to 7 full minutes without interruption.

Anatomical Prompt for Beginners: If you find it difficult to maintain the extended 8-second exhalation initially or feel a sense of air hunger, do not force your chest to strain. Shorten your cycle counts slightly to a comfortable 3-second nose inhalation linked to a smooth, quiet 6-second exhalation. The absolute priority is to keep your jaw, tongue, and throat completely soft and un-clenched across the practice.
