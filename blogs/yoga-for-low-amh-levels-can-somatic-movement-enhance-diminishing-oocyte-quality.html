The Complete, Detailed Guide to Your Daily Healing Somatic Asanas

1. Supported Reclined Butterfly Pose (Supta Baddha Konasana)

Time to Hold: Remain completely quiet in this shape for 10 to 12 minutes every single night right before sleep.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Sit down comfortably on your soft rug or directly on your bed mattress. Place a long, thick cushion or rolled blanket on the surface right behind your back. Slowly guide your torso down until your spine rests completely flat along the cushion's support. Bring the flat bottoms of your feet together to touch, and let your knees drop open gently toward the left and right sides. Slide extra pillows directly underneath your outer thighs and knees. This prop setup is vital so that your inner groin and lower stomach muscles do not feel any stretching pull, tension, or work. Rest your open palms gently on your lower abdomen, close your eyes, and allow your torso to become incredibly heavy.

Why it works: This passive hold eliminates all holding stress from your pelvic floor and lower core zones. It calms the sympathetic nerve networks wrapped around your reproductive organs, drops vascular resistance, and allows fresh blood to pool comfortably inside your ovarian pathways to optimize follicular fluid nutrition.

2. Supported Restorative Child's Pose (Salamba Balasana)

Time to Hold: Rest in this alignment for 6 to 8 minutes in the evening when you finish your workday.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Come down onto your knees on a comfortable mat or blanket. Place a thick, long bolster or two stacked bed pillows directly on the floor between your knees. Separate your knees wide to the outer edges of your mat, keeping your two big toes touching behind you. Slowly lower your torso down forward until your chest and your entire abdomen are resting completely flat along the length of your pillows. Turn your head softly to one comfortable side, rest your forearms easily on the floor alongside the pillows, and let your hips sink back heavily toward your heels. Close your eyes and feel your lower back expand with each inhalation.

Why it works: This position provides complete front-body support, which instantly signals your brain's fear centers to deactivate. It stretches and relaxes the deep sacral nerves, releases tension in the lower back muscles, and helps direct a calm wave of fresh arterial blood supply down into the lower abdomen.

3. Left-Nostril Soothing Breathwork (Chandra Bhedana Pranayama)

Time to Practice: Practice this calming breathwork for 5 to 7 minutes every morning before breakfast.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Sit up nice and tall in a comfortable cross-legged position on your bed or floor, placing a pillow beneath your hips to keep your spine straight and completely relaxed. Let your shoulders drop away from your ears. Rest your left hand easily on your knee. Bring your right hand up toward your face and gently close your eyes. Use your right thumb to softly close your right nostril. Inhale slowly and quietly through your open left nostril for a quiet count of 4 seconds. Then, close your left nostril with your ring finger, lift your thumb, and let all the air escape smoothly out through your right nostril for a count of 6 seconds. Continue this exact sequence: always breathing in through the left side, and always breathing out through the right side.

Why it works: This specialized breathwork activates your rest-and-restore parasympathetic pathways instantly. It helps clear systemic heat and cortisol from your system, stabilizes fluctuating blood pressures, and encourages vascular dilation across your deep internal organs.

4. Post-Meal Thunderbolt Posture (Vajrasana)

Time to Hold: Sit in this posture for 5 minutes immediately after your lunch and dinner.

Step-by-Step Instructions: Within 10 minutes of completing your meal, find a soft surface or mat. Kneel down on both knees. Bring your big toes together behind you so they touch, and separate your heels out wide to the sides, creating a comfortable space to sit your weight down between your feet. Keep your spine naturally straight, your chest open and light, and rest your palms flat on your thighs. Look straight ahead and breathe naturally, letting your lower abdomen move softly with every breath.

Why it works: Kneeling in Vajrasana alters the blood flow in your lower body, directing a warm wave of circulation away from your legs and straight into your digestive system. This steps up your digestive energy, prevents bloating, and helps your body process and clear out glucose from your food much more effectively.
