The Science Lesson: Sluggish Mitochondria, Insulin Spikes, and Pore Blockages

Let us explain how your internal cells talk to your face using very easy words. Every single cell in your body contains tiny power plants that take sugar from your food and turn it into smooth, active life energy.

When you experience slow cellular energy processing, your internal power plants become sluggish and less efficient. Because your cells are slow to open their doors, sugar stays stuck floating in your bloodstream. To fix this buildup, your pancreas is forced to release massive amounts of a helper hormone called insulin. This continuous flood of extra insulin travels throughout your body, sending an immediate signal to your ovaries to produce a sudden surge of testosterone and male tracking hormones.

This is where your face comes into the picture. The skin cells along your jawline are incredibly sensitive to testosterone signals. When that excess hormone wave hits your face, it changes the composition of your skin's natural protective oils, turning them into a thick, sticky glue. At the same time, high insulin levels speed up the growth of skin cells lining your pores, trapping that heavy oil deep beneath the surface. This dark, airless environment triggers local tissue inflammation, forming those deep, painful cysts along your jawline that stay swollen for weeks.
